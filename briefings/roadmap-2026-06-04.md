## 0. The reframe — what "done" now means

The earlier "done" question was *"which design-doc phase do we finish?"* That was
the wrong frame. The real driver arrived from the user's CSCO, who asked two
questions: **(1) what is an ontology, why/how, and (2) what is our agentic
strategy?** The user's strategy is to **answer by building the solution and
demonstrating it** — the running scenario is the argument.

So the milestone is no longer "finish the phases." It is:

> **A scenario a CSCO recognizes as "yeah, this is Tuesday," running on the
> ontology-driven generic-agent architecture, with a crisp explainer that makes
> the ontology + agentic strategy self-evident.**

The CSCO answers are crystallized in `briefings/csco-brief.md`. This roadmap is how
we make that brief *true on screen* and then write it up.

## 1. What the research settled (2026-06-04)

Four cited research threads (full findings in the session transcript) grounded the
scenario and the positioning. Verdict: **the narrative is real, and the gaps to
close are exactly the ones that also prove our thesis.**

- **Narrative validated.** 3.0× promo uplift is realistic (scanner data 2–8×);
  "sales commits / supply scrambles" is a $200B+ category where **59% of US promos
  lose money (72% global, McKinsey)**; "promo whiplash" **= the bullwhip effect**
  (price promos are a textbook root cause); the on-the-nose quote (r4.ai): *"no
  single system connects promotional intent to supply capability before commitments
  lock in."*
- **CSCO-credibility gaps = our Tier-1 work.** A CSCO would nod at the *situation*
  but flag three things in the *handling*: (a) it's a weekly **S&OE forum** with
  **role-owners** (plant scheduler, trade, sourcing) — we only have demand/supply
  planning; (b) **allocation / partial-fill** is the reflexive first move and is
  **missing**; (c) **co-man can't be a 1-week rescue** (slots book weeks out,
  qualification runs months, MOQ). Closing these is *also* the live proof of T4
  (role N+1 at ~zero cost). Realism and thesis-proof are the same work.
- **Positioning is crowded; differentiate below the tagline.** "Ontology-grounded
  agents" is now the **incumbent consensus** (o9 EKG, Blue Yonder Supply Chain
  Knowledge Graph + Inventory Ops Agent doing *our exact scene*, Palantir Ontology).
  Our defensible novelty: **the ontology renders agent identity/routing (zero
  per-role code)** + **agency-as-eval**. "Generic" is a **liability word**
  (Decklar sells against it) — always frame it as *generic runtime / role-agnostic
  code*, never *general-purpose LLM with no grounding*. The **static world model is
  our exposed flank** (the "where's the live data?" objection).

### Phase A — Scenario enrichment: CSCO-credible + prove T4  *(next)*

Make the scene one a CSCO recognizes, and let the realism double as the breadth
proof. **All §2-clean: facts and action-vocabulary in, decisions out.**

- **Data (ontology `world_state.yaml`)** — enrich to real magnitudes from the
  research cheat-sheet: multi-line plants (~50k cases/wk/line), mid-SKU demand
  ~1,500 cases/wk (matches Seed A baseline — already checks out), cases/pallet
  (Ti×Hi ≈ 48–50), OTIF terms (3% of COGS on non-compliant lines, 90/95/98 targets,
  $1k/mo waiver), co-man with **qualification status / MOQ / open-window** fields,
  lead times (material 2–8 wk, mfg 1–3 wk, transit 1–5 d). Present these as
  flat-file fixtures **behind the declared edges** — i.e. the integration points
  where SAP/real systems would plug in (the "how you'd connect this" story).
- **Roles (ontology)** — add **`plant_scheduler`** and **`trade`** roles. The DoD
  bar: they drop in with **zero edits to the agent template or the seven tools**
  (if they don't, the abstraction is leaking — stop, per the standing Phase-3 stop
  condition). This is the headline live demonstration of **T4** *and* the CSCO
  "who's in the room" fix.
- **Playbook (ontology)** — add the **`allocate_partial_fill`** path (the missing
  first-resort lever); **constrain `shift_to_coman`** with reader-readable gates
  (`qualified_for_sku`, `open_window`, `volume ≥ MOQ`) so it realistically *fails*
  a 1-week slip unless a qualified partner has open capacity. Keep the decision
  (which lever) as **agent judgment** — see §5 guardrail.
- **Orchestrator** — reader-tool implementations for the new world facts (mirror
  `application/reader_tools.py`); wire the new roles' role-views. No per-role code.
- **Terminology** — anchor "promo whiplash" → bullwhip once; adopt baseline/
  incremental, sell-in/sell-through, OTIF/MABD, S&OE.
- **§2 watch:** every addition states what is TRUE/LEGAL/POSSIBLE. No ranking,
  preference, threshold, or trade-off weight.
- **Static world model still holds** (single conflict; no two-decisions-on-shared-
  state yet — see Decision Point 1).
- **DoD:** a live run where the conflict is resolved through a realistic lever set
  with the new roles participating; co-man is correctly *rejected* when ungated;
  allocation appears as the first holding move; stub canonical-numbers traces green;
  a CSCO reading the trace says "this is how the call actually gets made."

## 3. Decision points (deferred — flag, don't stumble into)

1. **Tier-2 — live world model (project state from the event log).** Triggered if
   allocation spans **multiple retailers as interacting decisions on shared state**
   (the `[[static-world-model-deferral]]` trigger). This is the one move that turns
   our **biggest defensive weakness** (static fixture / "where's the live data?")
   into a feature — but it's also the biggest lift. **Decide after Phase A exists**,
   deliberately. Do not let allocation quietly grow into it.
2. **Portfolio triage (N simultaneous conflicts).** A CSCO lives in triage, not one
   clean conflict — so a single conflict always reads as *illustrative*. **User
   shelved this to a later phase.** Handle in the writeup with scope honesty: "we
   show the decision mechanics on one conflict; the architecture runs N in
   parallel." Don't pretend one conflict is the whole job.
3. **Line-magnitude realism (residual-capacity model) — DO BEFORE THE CSCO DEMO.**
   Surfaced in Phase A Session 1 (2026-06-04): the demo's line capacity (~5–6.5k
   cases/wk, conflict math locked, Seed-A baseline 1,500/wk load-bearing) reads as a
   *toy* to a CSCO — a real line runs ~30 SKUs at ~50k cases/wk. Fix is a **modeling
   reframe, not a brute rescale**: add a `committed_load` field so the line total is
   realistic (~50k), most is committed to other SKUs (~48.5k), and the **residual
   available stays ~1,500** — so the shortfall math *and* the Seed-A baseline survive
   untouched, while the conflict becomes *residual vs. promo-incremental* (the
   credible framing). Deliberately **deferred out of Phase A** because it's a
   coordinated cross-repo change (touches `test_world_state.py` + the orchestrator
   stub canonical numbers) → its **own paired mini-seed**, scheduled before the CSCO
   demo. Session 1 correctly kept the tuned numbers rather than break grounding
   mid-flight.

## 5. The §2 guardrail for the enrichment (the trap)

The resolution research is full of decision math — *"pick the lever minimizing
(OTIF penalty + co-man premium + lost margin)."* **That math is the agent's
judgment and must NOT be encoded.** Into the ontology/fixtures go the **facts** the
agent reads (penalty rate, premium %, MOQ, qualification status, open capacity,
promo flexibility). The **weighting / threshold / tie-break stays out** — encoding
"if penalty < premium then take the fine" is the §2 leak to refuse. Test for every
field: *does it say what is TRUE/LEGAL/POSSIBLE, or does it make the CHOICE?*
